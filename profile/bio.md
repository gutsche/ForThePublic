Oliver Gutsche is a Senior Scientist at Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab) and currently serves as Deputy for the Scientific Computing Division (SCD) in the Technology Directorate and Group Leader of the Storage Technology Group in SCD. He leads strategic and tactical initiatives to support Fermilab's scientific program with Software and Computing (S&C) solutions, including the Fermi Data Platform project to store and provide access to AI-ready datasets and Networking R&D projects with ESnet. He is member of the Science Council of the American Science Cloud, part of the Genesis mission of the DOE. Gutsche is member of the CMS experiment and currently serves as the Collaboration Board S&C sub-group co-convener. From 2019 to 2024, he led the U.S. CMS Software and Computing Operations Program — jointly funded by the Department of Energy (DOE) and the National Science Foundation (NSF) — overseeing the U.S. CMS Tier-1 facility at Fermilab and seven university Tier-2 sites. A key highlight of his career is his contribution to the observation of the Higgs boson with the CMS experiment at the Large Hadron Collider in 2012, where his work in scientific computing played a significant role. At heart, Gutsche is a particle physicist with research interests in searches for physics beyond the Standard Model, including Supersymmetry and Dark Matter. He holds a Diploma (2001) and a Doctorate (2005) in Physics, both from the University of Hamburg, Germany.
